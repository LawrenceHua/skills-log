# Concise professional format

## When to use

Use this skill for status updates, handoffs, summaries, and decision briefs that someone needs to scan quickly. Apply it to chat, email drafts, documents, and narrow-screen interfaces.

## Working method

1. **Write the first line as an answer.** Identify what the reader needs to know or decide. Lead with the outcome, recommendation, or decision request in one or two lines. If a decision has a deadline, put the exact date beside the request.
2. **Keep the first view short.** Aim for up to 120 words for a simple update and 250 for a complex brief. Keep the evidence or conditions that could change the reader's decision in the first view. Put supporting history, detailed comparisons, and full evidence in an expandable section, an appendix, or a linked artifact the reader can access. Give the full detail when the reader asks for it.
3. **Use the same fields for repeated items.** Give each item a title, one line for what it is, one line for why it matters or what it costs, and one line for the next action. Use the same order, labels, date format, and units for every item. For three or more comparable items, use a table if it fits the display. Use stacked entries when a table would make the reader scroll sideways.
4. **Keep the scope and evidence precise.** For a decision, name the exact action, its limits, and any condition that must hold before proceeding. For a status claim, name the check and what it observed. Label estimates and unknowns where they affect interpretation. A submission receipt supports a submission claim. A delivery claim needs evidence from the destination.
5. **Run three editing checks.** First, try to cut roughly a third of the draft and keep the shorter version if it preserves the decision and its supporting facts. Second, read only the first line of each section and scan repeated items for missing fields or inconsistent units. Third, preview the narrowest intended display. Check for horizontal scrolling, lengthy paragraphs, and controls that are hard to tap.

## How to use

1. Copy the `concise-professional-format` folder into `~/.claude/skills/` for personal use or into `.claude/skills/` inside a project.
2. Ask Claude Code to use the skill by name.

Example prompts

- "Use concise-professional-format to turn these release notes into a team update. Keep the checks we ran and the one open risk in the first view."
- "Use concise-professional-format to turn these four options into a brief I can approve from my phone. Keep the cost, deadline, and scope of approval visible."
